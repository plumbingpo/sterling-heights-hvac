Clear Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Before we touch a tool, we find out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check the age and tonnage of your system, the refrigerant charge, the heat exchanger and coils, and the condition of your ductwork. After that you get an honest recommendation and the complete price up front, whether the job is swapping a weak capacitor or replacing the whole system. No hidden fees, no surprise add-ons.

A lot of HVAC failures give quiet warnings long before the system quits. Watch for energy bills that creep up, airflow that feels weaker than it used to, frost on the copper lines, or a bedroom that never catches up to the thermostat. Behind those symptoms are usually pinhole refrigerant leaks, worn blower bearings, or a capacitor on its way out. A quick inspection at the first sign often costs far less than a failed compressor.

Not every call is an emergency. Many Sterling Heights homeowners ask us to evaluate an aging system before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical AC or furnace lifespan of 12 to 18 years, replacing on your own schedule beats scrambling for a new unit during a cold snap. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your equipment has left.

Maintenance Plans

Tune-Ups That Keep Your System Running

The best repair is the one you never need. That's the whole point of our seasonal plans. Twice a year, we clean coils and burners, check the refrigerant charge, test the electrical components, inspect the heat exchanger, flush the condensate drain, and change the filter, all matched to the equipment in your home.

Many homes do best with two visits a year: a spring tune-up for cooling and a fall tune-up for heating. The schedule flexes with the age of your equipment, how hard your household runs it, and local conditions like dust and pollen, which coat condenser coils and push up energy use fast.

Consider maintenance as protection for equipment you've already bought. Each tune-up helps keep the coils clean, the charge correct, and the filter fresh, which lowers monthly energy bills while easing the strain on the compressor and blower. For most families, a plan costs far less than replacing the system ahead of schedule.

Proper Sizing

The Right Size System for Your Home

When it comes to HVAC, bigger is not better. An oversized unit short-cycles, wastes energy, and never runs long enough to pull humidity out of the air, while an undersized one runs nonstop and still can't keep up on the hottest days. We don't simply copy the tonnage of the old unit. We run a real load calculation that factors in your square footage, insulation, windows, and ductwork, and for mini-splits we size each head to the room it serves.

Homes change over time. A finished basement, an addition, or two new bedrooms can overwhelm the single-stage system that used to be plenty. Today's two-stage and variable-speed equipment holds tighter temperatures and uses much less energy. If you're torn between a heat pump and a furnace-and-AC pair, or ducted and ductless, we'll lay out the honest pros and cons in SEER2 ratings, operating cost, electrical and venting needs, and installation work.

Frequent Failures

Heating and Cooling Problems We Solve in Sterling Heights

Most service calls we take in Sterling Heights come down to a handful of recurring problems. The AC runs but the air never gets cold. The furnace won't light, or it lights and shuts off minutes later. The evaporator coil freezes solid, the outdoor unit hums but never kicks on, or a mini-split starts flashing error codes. Add refrigerant leaks, clogged drains, and forgetful thermostats, and you have the full list. Our technicians handle all of them, starting with the exact part that failed.

Not every HVAC problem is equally urgent. A bad capacitor just keeps the compressor from starting. A cracked heat exchanger, on the other hand, can let combustion gases into the house, and a backed-up condensate drain can quietly ruin a ceiling before anyone notices. Our technicians carry the parts to swap capacitors, contactors, fan motors, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards, repair leaky ducts that dump conditioned air into the attic, and recharge refrigerant once a leak is fixed.
